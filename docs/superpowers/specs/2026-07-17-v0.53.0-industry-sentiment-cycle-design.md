# v0.53.0 Industry Sentiment Cycle Design

Date: 2026-07-17
Status: approved direction; implementation not started
Scope: deterministic industry sentiment, cycle phase, and experimental turning-risk forecast

## 1. Problem

The existing market-intelligence report combines industry price trend, mapped news,
keywords, and institutional flow, but it does not produce a comparable industry mood
score or preserve a daily mood history. Users therefore cannot answer these questions
consistently:

- Which industries have the strongest or weakest current mood?
- Is an industry's mood warming or cooling relative to its 20-day baseline?
- Is the move in ignition, expansion, overheating, cooling, capitulation, or recovery?
- Is there evidence that a sentiment peak or trough may occur soon?
- Which inputs produced the score, and is the result trustworthy?

This feature must remain descriptive research support. It must not claim to predict an
exact stock-price top, bottom, date, or investment return.

## 2. Product research findings

Public descriptions of the CMoney / Uncle Stock app show a workflow built around:

1. Determine the broad-market regime from the weighted and OTC indices.
2. Rank stocks and industries by a proprietary mood value.
3. Track ranking duration, volume, branch and institutional buying, forecast revenue or
   EPS, and technical breakouts.
4. Treat a newly ranked item differently from an item that has remained ranked for more
   than five days or has become extremely crowded.
5. Use historical mood charts and an industry-chain map to find where attention and money
   are concentrating.

The public sources do not disclose the proprietary formula or publish evidence that it
predicts exact sentiment or price turning points. This design adopts the observable product
principles--ranking, persistence, breadth, confirmation, divergence, and exhaustion--but
uses an original, auditable methodology.

## 3. Goals

- Produce a deterministic 5-day industry sentiment score from -100 to +100.
- Produce a comparable 20-day baseline and a warming or cooling delta.
- Preserve the selected weights: news 40%, price 30%, institutional flow 30%.
- Expand official TWSE and TPEx institutional-flow collection to 20 trading days.
- Assign an explainable cycle phase.
- Preserve one daily snapshot per industry for later validation.
- Produce experimental 1-day and 5-day sentiment projections when history is sufficient.
- Produce peak-risk and trough-risk scores and a possible turning window without claiming
  an exact point.
- Expose inputs, contributions, confidence, missing-data reasons, and methodology version.
- Leave a typed extension point for a future LLM news reviewer without requiring an LLM.

## 4. Non-goals

- Reproduce or reverse-engineer another product's proprietary formula.
- Predict exact stock prices, price targets, or guaranteed turning dates.
- Emit buy, sell, position-size, or allocation instructions.
- Let an LLM silently overwrite the deterministic score.
- Train or promote a machine-learning forecast before walk-forward validation passes.
- Treat high sentiment as equivalent to high expected return.

## 5. Architecture

### 5.1 New sentiment module

Add `src/taiwan_stock_analysis/industry_sentiment.py`. It owns pure calculations and has
no network or filesystem side effects. Its public responsibilities are:

- score mapped news for one industry and time window;
- score price momentum, breadth, and volume confirmation;
- score institutional-flow persistence relative to traded volume;
- combine available components with explicit effective weights;
- classify label, temperature, confidence, and cycle phase;
- project the sentiment series when snapshot history is sufficient;
- calculate experimental peak and trough risk;
- return structured reasons and diagnostics.

### 5.2 Market-intelligence integration

`market_intelligence.py` remains the orchestration and reporting layer. It will:

1. map news and flow rows to industries as it does today;
2. load the price-trend payload and sentiment snapshot history;
3. call the pure sentiment module once per industry;
4. embed the returned `sentiment` object in each industry row;
5. write the current snapshot to the history store after the report is built;
6. render sentiment in JSON, Markdown, standalone HTML, and the main dashboard.

### 5.3 Official flow-history adapters

The market-data importer will add date-driven TWSE and TPEx adapters that collect the most
recent 20 trading sessions. Calendar days with no official rows are skipped. Rows are
deduplicated by `(date, stock_id, source)` and sorted deterministically.

The output `fund_flow.csv` remains backward compatible but may contain multiple dates.
Collection errors are recorded per market and date. A partial result is retained rather
than discarded.

### 5.4 Future LLM extension point

Define a `NewsSentimentReviewer` protocol with one method that accepts normalized news and
the deterministic assessment, and returns a separate review payload. The default reviewer
is absent. A future implementation may add `llm_review`, but it must not change
`deterministic_score` unless a later version adds an explicit, tested promotion policy.

## 7. Deterministic scoring

All component scores are clamped to `[-100, 100]`. Intermediate values are retained at full
precision and displayed values are rounded to one decimal place.

### 7.1 News component

- Use a versioned Traditional Chinese financial lexicon with positive, negative, negation,
  and intensity terms.
- Normalize text with Unicode NFKC, lowercase Latin text, and collapse whitespace. Tokenize
  with deterministic longest-match-first scanning against the versioned phrase lexicon;
  unmatched characters become single-character tokens. No runtime-dependent statistical
  tokenizer is used in v1.
- Score title and summary separately; title has weight 0.65 and summary has weight 0.35.
- Apply a negation sign flip within three tokens and cap intensity multiplication at 2.0.
- Deduplicate by canonical URL, falling back to normalized title.
- Use exponential recency weighting with a 3-day half-life for the 5-day score and a 7-day
  half-life for the 20-day score.
- Each source contributes at most 40% of the total news weight for an industry, preventing
  a single feed from dominating.
- The 40% cap uses the uncapped recency-weight total as its denominator. Weight clipped
  from an overrepresented source is left as neutral, unallocated coverage rather than
  redistributed; this keeps the cap mathematically valid when only one or two sources are
  available and emits a source-concentration warning.
- Fewer than three mapped articles in a window creates a low-coverage warning.

The component score is the recency- and source-capped weighted mean of article scores.
News count, novelty, and repeated-topic concentration are diagnostics used by turning-risk
logic; they do not make positive news more positive merely because it is repeated.
Repeated-topic concentration is the largest share of 5-day articles assigned to one
normalized event-keyword group by the existing keyword pipeline. An article with no event
keyword receives its own unique group, so unrelated stories cannot be grouped merely
because they map to the same industry.

### 7.2 Price component

Extend industry trends with:

- positive-return breadth for 5 and 20 sessions;
- average 5-day volume ratio;
- count of stocks at a 20-day high or low;
- industry coverage ratios.

For window `w`:

```text
return_term = clamp(industry_return_w / return_scale_w, -1, 1)
breadth_term = 2 * positive_breadth_w - 1
volume_term = sign(industry_return_w) * clamp(average_volume_ratio_5d - 1, 0, 1)
price_score_w = 100 * clamp(
    0.60 * return_term + 0.25 * breadth_term + 0.15 * volume_term,
    -1,
    1
)
```

`return_scale_5d` is 8% and `return_scale_20d` is 15%. These bootstrap scales are versioned
constants and must be reviewed against walk-forward evidence before later changes.

### 7.3 Institutional-flow component

For each industry and window:

```text
flow_ratio_w = sum(total institutional net shares_w) / sum(traded shares_w)
persistence_w = (buy_days - sell_days) / valid_flow_days
flow_score_w = 100 * clamp(
    0.75 * (flow_ratio_w / 0.05) + 0.25 * persistence_w,
    -1,
    1
)
```

`total institutional net shares` is the sum of the official foreign-investor,
investment-trust, and dealer net-share fields after the existing stock-ID normalization.

The numerator and denominator use only `(date, stock_id)` pairs for which both official
institutional flow and positive traded-share volume exist. A session is valid when these
joined pairs cover at least 60% of the industry's price-covered mapped stocks. Unjoined rows
are excluded rather than treated as zero and are listed in diagnostics. The 5-day window is
the latest five expected trading sessions and requires at least three valid days; the 20-day
window is the latest twenty and requires at least ten. The report must expose valid days,
missing dates, joined and expected stocks, market coverage, net shares, traded shares, and
persistence.

### 7.4 Composite and missing data

Configured weights are:

```text
news = 0.40
price = 0.30
fund_flow = 0.30
```

At least two fresh components are required. When exactly two are available, their configured
weights are renormalized to one and returned as `effective_weight`; the score is marked
`partial` and confidence cannot be high. Fewer than two components produces
`insufficient_data` and no numeric composite.

```text
score_5d = weighted mean of available 5-day component scores
baseline_20d = weighted mean of available 20-day component scores
change = score_5d - baseline_20d
```

Temperature is `warming` when change is at least +10, `cooling` when at most -10, and
`stable` otherwise.

Labels are:

- `extremely_optimistic`: score >= 60
- `optimistic`: 20 <= score < 60
- `neutral`: -20 < score < 20
- `pessimistic`: -60 < score <= -20
- `extremely_pessimistic`: score <= -60

## 8. Confidence

Confidence is based on source freshness and coverage, not on the absolute score.

Freshness is evaluated against the latest completed Taiwan market session in the report:
price and flow are fresh when their latest valid row is from that session or the immediately
preceding expected session; news is fresh when at least one mapped article was published in
the previous 48 hours. A holiday does not create staleness merely because it adds calendar
days.

- `high`: all three components are fresh; news has at least five 5-day articles; price
  coverage is at least 80%; flow has at least four valid 5-day sessions.
- `medium`: at least two components are fresh and no required component has a source error.
- `low`: two components are usable but one has weak coverage or a partial-source warning.
- no confidence label is emitted when fewer than two components are usable.

Every downgrade must add a human-readable warning.

## 9. Cycle phase

Phase calculations use these fixed v1 diagnostics:

- `recent_slope` is the ordinary least-squares slope of the latest three valid daily
  composite snapshots, including the current snapshot. It is positive at `>= +2` sentiment
  points per session, negative at `<= -2`, and flat otherwise. It is unavailable with fewer
  than three snapshots.
- breadth is expanding when `breadth_5d - breadth_20d >= 0.10` and contracting when the
  difference is `<= -0.10`.
- a crowding warning exists when any of these holds: top-quartile ranking streak `>= 5`,
  repeated-topic news concentration `>= 60%`, or average 5-day volume ratio `>= 1.8` while
  the absolute composite score is at least 60.
- a deceleration warning exists when the score is at least 50, the prior three-snapshot
  slope was positive, and the current slope has fallen by at least 50%; the negative-score
  rule is symmetric.

Rules that require `recent_slope` do not fire when it is unavailable. This intentionally
falls back to `consolidation` instead of manufacturing a trend from the 5-day/20-day delta.

Rules are evaluated in the following order so phases cannot overlap:

1. `overheating`: score >= 70, or trailing percentile >= 90 with ranking streak >= 3 days,
   plus at least one crowding or deceleration warning.
2. `capitulation`: score <= -60 and the recent slope is negative.
3. `recovery`: score <= 20, change >= 10, and recent slope is positive.
4. `ignition`: -20 <= score < 40, change >= 10, recent slope is positive, and breadth is
   expanding.
5. `expansion`: 20 <= score < 70, recent slope is positive, and 5-day breadth >= 55%.
6. `cooling`: score > -20 and either change <= -10 or recent slope is negative while
   breadth contracts.
7. `consolidation`: none of the above.

Ranking streak counts consecutive snapshots in which the industry is in the top sentiment
quartile. It is not reconstructed from future information.

## 10. Forecast and turning-risk shadow mode

### 10.1 Snapshot history

Write one row per `(as_of_date, category, methodology_version)` to
`market-intelligence/industry_sentiment_history.csv`. Re-running the same date replaces the
same key deterministically instead of appending a duplicate.

The history stores composite and component scores, breadth, volume, flow persistence,
news novelty, rank, cycle phase, and confidence. It does not store future labels at runtime.

### 10.2 Sentiment projection

With at least 20 valid snapshot days, fit an exponentially weighted linear trend to the most
recent ten scores. Apply a mean-reversion term toward the current 20-day baseline:

```text
daily_step = 0.70 * forecast_slope_10d + 0.30 * ((baseline_20d - score_5d) / 20)
forecast_h = clamp(score_5d + h * daily_step, -100, 100)
```

The exponential weights have a three-session half-life. The forecast interval uses the
one-step residuals from the same available history: `robust_sigma = 1.4826 * MAD`, and the
displayed interval is `forecast_h +/- 1.96 * robust_sigma * sqrt(h)`, clamped to
`[-100, 100]`. With fewer than ten residuals, the point projection is shown but the interval
is omitted with an explicit warning. This is a deterministic projection, not a calibrated
probability.

History gates:

- fewer than 20 valid days: `insufficient_history`;
- 20-59 valid days: experimental 1-day and 5-day projection, no turning window;
- 60 or more valid days: experimental projection and turning-risk window;
- calibrated status requires the promotion gates below.

### 10.3 Peak and trough risk

Turning risk is computed only with at least 60 valid snapshots. Define:

```text
slope_now = (score_t - score_t_minus_2) / 2
slope_prior = (score_t_minus_3 - score_t_minus_5) / 2
level_peak = 15 * clamp((score_t - 50) / 30, 0, 1)
           + 10 * clamp((percentile_t - 80) / 15, 0, 1)
level_trough = 15 * clamp((-score_t - 40) / 30, 0, 1)
             + 10 * clamp((20 - percentile_t) / 15, 0, 1)
momentum_peak = 25 * clamp(
    (slope_prior - slope_now) / max(abs(slope_prior), 2), 0, 1
) when slope_prior > 0, otherwise 0
momentum_trough = 25 * clamp(
    (slope_now - slope_prior) / max(abs(slope_prior), 2), 0, 1
) when slope_prior < 0, otherwise 0
breadth_peak = 20 * clamp((breadth_20d - breadth_5d) / 0.20, 0, 1)
               when score_t > 20, otherwise 0
breadth_trough = 20 * clamp((breadth_5d - breadth_20d) / 0.20, 0, 1)
                 when score_t < -20, otherwise 0
flow_peak = 15 * clamp((flow_score_20d - flow_score_5d) / 40, 0, 1)
            when score_t > 20, otherwise 0
flow_trough = 15 * clamp((flow_score_5d - flow_score_20d) / 40, 0, 1)
              when score_t < -20, otherwise 0
```

`percentile_t` uses the prior 60 valid snapshots plus the current snapshot; future values
are never used. Peak crowding contributes five points for each true condition: top-quartile
streak `>= 5`, repeated positive-topic concentration `>= 60%`, and volume ratio `>= 1.8`
with positive return. Trough capitulation is symmetric: bottom-quartile streak, repeated
negative-topic concentration, and high volume with negative return. Each family is capped
at its stated maximum.

Peak risk is the 0-100 sum of:

- extreme positive level or historical percentile: 0-25;
- positive slope losing momentum: 0-25;
- price or stock breadth deteriorating before the composite: 0-20;
- institutional flow reversal: 0-15;
- crowding, news repetition, or long top-quartile streak: 0-15.

Trough risk uses the symmetric negative-level, downside-deceleration, breadth-recovery,
selling-exhaustion, and capitulation diagnostics.

Until calibration, these values are named `risk`, never `probability`. The turning window is:

- `1_to_3_days` when risk >= 70 and at least three diagnostic families agree;
- `4_to_7_days` when risk is 50-69 and at least two families agree;
- `unclear` otherwise.

A family agrees when its contribution reaches 40% of that family's maximum: level and
momentum at 10 points, breadth at 8, flow at 6, and crowding/capitulation at 6. The output
also includes `direction`: `peak` or `trough` for the qualifying side with higher risk, and
`unclear` when neither side qualifies or both sides exceed 50.

Peak and trough risk may both be low. If both exceed 50 because inputs conflict, the window
is `unclear` and a regime-uncertainty warning is required.

### 10.4 Backtest labels and promotion

Backtest-only labels use future data and must never leak into runtime features:

- a sentiment peak occurs when the current score is the local maximum in a +/-5-session
  window and the following five sessions fall by at least 15 points;
- a sentiment trough is the symmetric local minimum followed by a rise of at least 15 points.

Validation uses expanding-window, walk-forward evaluation by date. Random train/test splits
are prohibited.

Promotion from `experimental` to `calibrated` requires all of:

- at least 252 market sessions of snapshots;
- at least 30 peak and 30 trough events in the pooled validation universe;
- no look-ahead or revised-input leakage in an automated audit;
- Brier score better than the unconditional event-rate baseline for both targets;
- precision and recall reported at the 50 and 70 risk thresholds;
- results stable in at least two non-overlapping holdout periods;
- a written model-validity report.

Failure to pass leaves the output in shadow mode; thresholds are not tuned merely to obtain
a passing result.

## 11. Dashboard and reports

Each industry card will show:

- current 5-day score and color band;
- 20-day baseline and change arrow;
- confidence and data freshness;
- cycle phase;
- three component contributions;
- history sparkline;
- experimental 1-day and 5-day projection with interval, when available;
- peak and trough risk with an explicit `experimental` badge;
- top three reasons and warnings.

Sorting supports current score, warming rate, peak risk, trough risk, and confidence. The
default remains current score. Color is never the sole carrier of meaning.

Stable hooks include:

- `data-industry-sentiment`
- `data-industry-sentiment-score`
- `data-industry-sentiment-phase`
- `data-industry-sentiment-confidence`
- `data-industry-sentiment-forecast`
- `data-industry-turning-risk`

## 12. Quality gates and failure behavior

- Missing or stale inputs degrade confidence and name the affected component.
- Fewer than two usable components suppress the composite rather than inventing a score.
- Insufficient history suppresses projections and turning windows without blocking the
  current score.
- Source errors are retained in the report and do not silently become neutral values.
- All scores remain bounded and all effective weights sum to one when a score exists.
- Reports preserve the non-advice notice and describe turning outputs as research signals.

## 13. Testing

- Unit tests for lexicon scoring, negation, intensity, source caps, and deduplication.
- Unit tests for every formula boundary and missing-data combination.
- Unit tests for phase precedence and turning-risk diagnostic agreement.
- Tests proving that runtime features never read future snapshots.
- Deterministic fixture tests for TWSE and TPEx 20-session flow collection.
- Schema tests for JSON, Markdown, HTML, snapshot history, and dashboard hooks.
- CLI integration tests with no network dependency.
- Walk-forward backtest tests with synthetic known peaks and troughs.
- Browser QA at desktop and 375px widths, including non-color status labels.
- Full existing test suite, `compileall`, `doctor demo`, and `git diff --check`.
